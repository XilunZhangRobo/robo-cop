Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies trained on large datasets are capable within their training domains, yet they still fail to generalize to the variety of situations a robot meets in real-world deployment. Agentic robot systems complement the policy with a vision-language model (VLM) orchestrator that learns when to call the policy, how to instruct it, and when to use scripted skills instead. However, because the harness is built around a frozen policy that has limited language steerability, the orchestrator can avoid the policy's failures but never overcome them. The policy becomes the bottleneck of the whole system. Fine-tuning the policy can remove this bottleneck, but updating it alone decouples it from an orchestrator tuned to its old behavior. We propose Robo-COP, in which the orchestrator and policy co-evolve during deployment. Robo-COP curates skill demonstrations from its own executions, fine-tunes the policy when this data can address recurring failures, and adopts each new policy only after it improves the skills it was trained for. Across ten simulated RoboLab tasks, Robo-COP raises mean held-out success from 64.8% to 73.8% over the same harness with a frozen policy, while fine-tuning on a fixed schedule without verification reaches only 65.8%. On three real-world tasks, Robo-COP raises held-out success from 38.3% to 50.0%. Robo-COP turns deployment into a self-improving flywheel in which robots learn by doing, with each improvement in execution producing better data for the next round of learning. Videos and code are available at https://robo-cop.pages.dev/.

Method

The orchestrator is the language model that writes robot programs. The harness is the set of tools, task memory, and execution loop around it.

The agent can call the policy to pick up an object, or use perception to find a grasp and move the arm with scripted primitives. A visual monitor checks execution and sends feedback when revised code is needed.

Episode reflection updates task memory. Policy actions and scripted motions are recorded in a shared action stream. The curator extracts skill demonstrations from successful and failed episodes and checks them before adding them to the training data.

A controller decides when to train and names target skills for verification. Training uses all curated data accumulated so far. Verification checks task success, previously admitted skills, and whether the candidate itself performed its targets.

After adoption or reversion, advice that discourages VLA use is marked unverified for the policy now in service. Its history is kept, so the orchestrator can retest the skill rather than assume the old advice still applies.

Skill demonstration curation

A text judge labels skills from code and execution feedback. A video judge checks the proposed labels. Segments are kept when the text judge accepts them and the video shows success or useful partial progress.

Simulation results

After deployment, each system is tested on 50 new initializations per task with no further updates. Mean success is 73.8% for Robo-COP, 64.8% for the frozen-policy control, and 65.8% for fixed-schedule updates. ASPIRE reaches 54.0%; the standalone VLA reaches 28.0%.
